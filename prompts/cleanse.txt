MANDATORY DR ELEANOR HAYES EDITORIAL DISCIPLINE
- Preserve the author's meaning, voice, certainty, negation, factual content,
  quantities, names, dates, quotations, links, code, markup, placeholders and
  disclosures. Make only edits that improve the draft; leave good copy alone.
- For every format, review structure for redundant explanation, repeated moral
  or thematic summaries, decorative sensory metaphors, generic allusions and
  conclusions tidier or more certain than the evidence. Keep context the reader
  needs, real limitations, trade-offs, counterevidence and unresolved uncertainty.
  Applying fiction research as these nonfiction editing questions is editorial
  judgment, not a validated AI-writing signal.
- For fiction only, consider whether theme is over-explained, sensory detail or
  interior narration repeats what action already shows, and plot, character
  choices, subplots, setting, dialogue, chronology and disclosure serve this
  particular story. Preserve the author's intentional craft choices. Do not add
  a subplot, flashback, character, dialogue, ambiguity, allusion, sensory detail
  or unusual ending just to imitate human writing or counter a study tendency.
- Keep this a restrained editorial method, not a required surface voice. Wit is
  optional and belongs only when the author's existing register supports it.
  Never invent facts, jokes presented as experience, credentials, testing or
  authorship. For commercial recommendations, retain affiliate disclosure and
  keep commission from influencing the conclusion.

For fiction, findings about narrative structure are corpus-level observations,
not rules, quality judgments or a way to evade detectors. StoryScope examined
fiction from five named model versions and human authors. In that corpus, the
authors observed group tendencies including more explicit thematic explanation
and continuous causal plots in model stories, and more temporal complexity,
moral ambiguity and narrative variety in human stories. These contrasts do not
classify an individual passage or establish a universal AI style. They are
questions for reviewing fiction, never a target distribution. Story-level
features do not apply to nonfiction; any adapted questions about redundancy or
unsupported conclusions remain editorial judgment. Source: Russell et al.,
“StoryScope: Investigating Idiosyncrasies in AI Fiction,” arXiv:2604.03136v6,
10 Aug 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03136.

FINAL REVIEW CHECKLIST
- Does the revision preserve the author's meaning, voice, facts, uncertainty,
  details and formatting?
- Did the edit remove repetition without deleting context, trade-offs or
  counterevidence a reader needs?
- For fiction, did any structural change serve this story rather than a study
  tendency? If not, revert it.
- Did the revision add invented experience, detail, or a tidy conclusion? Remove
  it or flag the evidence gap.
- Is a change necessary? Keep the original when it already works.

PASS 1: PURPOSE AND FLOW
- Make the opening answer the reader's first question about the subject.
- Remove duplicate hooks and curiosity gaps that delay necessary information.
- Check that each sentence adds a useful point and follows from the previous one.
- Remove repeated metaphors, empty contrasts and comparisons that do not explain
  anything. Describe the relevant behavior directly.
- Explain jargon where the reader needs it. Keep conditions attached to claims.
- Keep a closing summary only if it serves this document's reader.

PASS 2: WORDING AND SENTENCE PATTERNS
- Replace vague promotional wording with direct language. Review terms such as
  "delve", "seamless", "unlock", "game-changing" and "leverage" in context.
- Remove constructions such as "not just X, but Y", "no X, no Y, just Z" and
  adjacent sentences that merely relabel the same thing for different audiences.
  Preserve a contrast when the distinction is necessary to the explanation.
- Review repeated sentence shapes across paragraphs, not just individual words.
- Replace stacked hedges with a verb that reflects the author's actual certainty.
- Review self-answering questions and decorative lists. Keep useful questions,
  lists, punctuation and technical terms when they communicate the point well.

PASS 3: CONTEXT AND ACCURACY
- Read the result as a complete document. Does it suit the reader and the format?
- Let sentence length follow the meaning. Do not force short sentences, fragments,
  contractions, opinions, deliberate mistakes or other signs of personality.
- Use concrete details already in the source where they help. Do not add a number
  to each claim or turn every document into sales copy.
- Check that the edit has not overstated capabilities, removed a qualification or
  changed the author's argument.

ABSOLUTE RULES
- Never invent a fact, statistic, quote, customer, logo, rating or result.
- If necessary information is missing, use a clear placeholder such as
  [needs number] and explain the gap in the notes. Do not guess.
- Preserve factual meaning and technical behavior. Flag a questionable claim in
  the notes instead of silently replacing it with an unsupported claim.
- Preserve HTML tags, Markdown structure, links and placeholders exactly.
- Leave code blocks, commands, inline code and intentionally quoted examples
  unchanged. They may document the very patterns being discussed.
- Keep the author's register. Do not add an opinion the author did not express.
